# Chapter 6: The Lights We Keep

## Chapter Summary
Cornered into publicly agreeing to play guitar for the group's physics-song project, Damien gets folded into his
friends' orbit whether he likes it or not — a shared cup of coconut juice, a Saturday trip to his old house. When
the visit edges toward something real (his grandparents, the house no one lives in), Damien reaches for the same
instinct that got him into this mess in the first place: deflection. This time it's a prank instead of a joke,
and it works a little too well.

## Narrative Objectives
* Show Damien being pulled into the group's rhythm through small, low-stakes belonging (the coconut juice, the
venue banter) rather than a big dramatic gesture.
* Land one clear, silent character beat: Damien shutting down Mateo's joke about Dahlia without a word.
* Plant a structural echo — Damien deflects with humor twice in this chapter (the guitar "yes," and the prank)
— without naming the pattern outright.
* Give the reader real (if brief) tenderness around the grandparents' house before undercutting it with the prank,
so the prank reads as Damien protecting himself, not just as a gag.

## Scene List
1. **06a — Agreeing Damien as Guitarist:** Mateo outs the joke publicly; Damien is cornered into committing for real.
2. **06b — After Noon After Literature:** The group locks in at four (Ben opts out), the venue gets settled,
coconut juice on the walk home.
3. **06c — Going to the Old House:** Flight to the old neighborhood, the house itself, the grandparents mentioned
— and Damien flicks the lights.

# Scene: Agreeing Damien as Guitarist

## Context
* **Location:** Classroom
* **Setting:** Morning, before Ms. Shiela's physics class
* **Characters:** Damien, Mateo, Elena, Ben, Joan; Dahlia present in the background (not a speaking part)

## Objective
* Take Damien's private, half-said "I guess I can play" from Chapter 5 and force it into a public commitment he
can no longer walk back with "I was just messing with Mateo."
* Deliver one silent character beat: Damien shutting down Mateo's joke about Dahlia with a look, not a line.

## Key Beats
* Mateo announces it to the group before Damien can control the framing.
    ```
        Mateo: Hey guys, Damien just told me yesterday he can play for us in the task!
        Elena: Wow, so you can play instruments?
        Damien: An instrument.
        Damien: Wait, no — I was just messing with Mateo yesterday!
        Joan: Lemme guess, violin?
        Damien: Guitar.
        Damien: But still, no. I can't. I'm just a beginner.
        Joan: Oh come on, everyone has a "beginner's phase"!
        Elena: Besides, we can pick easier songs for you to play.
        Damien: Or you could just save an mp3 instrumental. Problem solved, right?
        Ben: Live performance will probably sound better, though.
        Mateo: Besides...
    ```
* **The Dahlia beat (new):** Mateo glances toward Dahlia mid-sentence — "Girls like that, you know" — and
trails off before finishing. Damien doesn't respond verbally. He just looks at Mateo, flat and unimpressed, long
enough that Mateo visibly reads it as *not funny.* Mateo breaks eye contact first, clears his throat, and steers
back to the actual topic without acknowledging the misstep out loud. No one else in the group seems to clock it
— this is between Damien and Mateo only.
    ```
        *Mateo glances toward Dahlia*
        Mateo: Girls like that... you know—
        *Damien looks at him, flat, unimpressed. A beat too long.*
        *Mateo clears his throat and looks away first.*
        Mateo: —anyway. So. You in or not?
        *Damien exhales through his nose, thinks for a moment.*
        Damien inner monologue: As if I can stop these people.
        Damien: Fine. But I get to pick the song.
    ```
* Background blocking during the conversation (keep from original): Joan and Elena scrolling their phones, Ben
checking his braces in a pocket mirror, Mateo half-lying across his desk.
* Damien's inner monologue as the bell/class settles: *Jeez, I hope they don't pick something with barre chords.*
* Ms. Shiela enters; class starts.
    ```
        Ms. Shiela: Newton's laws — the apple guy, as you already know...
        (brief lesson beat, kept short)
    ```
* Damien glances at his own fingertips on his left hand. Joan notices.
    ```
        Joan: That's gonna hurt for a few weeks — months, maybe. But worth it once the calluses set in.
    ```
* Ms. Shiela, mid-lesson, addresses the class generally — not aimed at Damien specifically, just landing near him:
    ```
        Ms. Shiela: By the way — if I were you, I'd be taking more notes. Gives you more to say in your song project.
    ```
* Joan and Elena, seated across the room, catch Damien's eye and mime cheering/gesture that he's carrying the group.
* Ms. Shiela announces the second-quarter capstone project; Mateo groans about it being "college stuff."
    ```
        Ms. Shiela: After that project, you'll also start on a capstone project for the second quarter.
        *Mateo raises his hand*
        Mateo: Excuse me, ma'am — do we really have to do that? I thought capstone stuff was for college...
        Ms. Shiela: Good thing you'll already have the experience, then. Come college, you'll be ready.
    ```
* *(Cut: Damien's inner monologue about school "preparing you to be prepared" — trimmed per revision.)*

## Outcome
* Damien is now committed in front of the whole group — there's no more "it was a joke" exit available to him.
* The Dahlia beat plants a quiet thread: Damien will shut down a joke at her expense without needing anyone to
ask him to, and without explaining why.

# Scene: After Noon After Literature

## Context
* **Location:** Classroom, then the road home
* **Setting:** End of the school day
* **Characters:** Damien, Mateo, Elena, Ben, Joan; Sir Marco (brief, exiting)

## Objective
* Resolve the group down to four (Damien, Mateo, Elena, Joan) by having Ben opt out cleanly, so the reader
understands why Ben isn't part of Saturday's scene.
* Land the venue decision at Damien's old house through banter, not through Damien volunteering it earnestly.
* Give a small, wordless beat of belonging (the coconut juice) that contrasts with Damien's usual guardedness —
he doesn't perform this one, he just lets it happen.

## Key Beats
* Sir Marco dismisses class.
    ```
        Everyone: Goodbye, sir.
        Sir Marco: Goodbye, class — bring your own pad papers next time, because next week I'm not letting anyone
        borrow from classmates.
        Everyone: Oh, come on...
    ```
* Most of the class filters out; Damien's group gathers at his desk.
    ```
        Elena: Guys, we forgot to count heads — Ms. Shiela said four per group, but we're five here. So either
        someone's group is short, or—
        *Ben cuts in*
        Ben: I already got my own group, don't worry.
        Mateo: Wait, why didn't you tell us?
        Joan: When did you even plan this?
        Elena: Which group? Are you sure?
        *Damien isn't surprised. He keeps packing his bag.*
        *Ben scratches the back of his head.*
        Ben: I'll go with Reynold's group. I mean — Elena and Mateo can sing, Joan and Damien can play. I'm
        tone-deaf. I don't want to be the reason it falls apart.
        *Damien stands, starts closing the windows.*
        Mateo: It's a physics project, man, not a music subject — nobody's grading us that hard.
        *Damien gives Ben's arm a light punch on the way past.*
        Damien: You're too serious, dude.
        Joan: It's fine, if Ben's already got plans with the other guys.
    ```
* They step out and shut the classroom door behind them.
    ```
        Joan: Anyway, we can't record at my place on weekends — my mom doesn't like a lot of people in the house.
        Damien: We can go to my place.
        Mateo: Oh-oh-oh — you heard him, guys, he said his place!
        Damien: I'm suggesting. Not requesting.
        Elena: I'm fine with Damien's place.
        Joan: Yeah, same.
        Mateo: Okay. Case closed.
    ```
* On the walk home — Ben, Mateo, and Damien.
    ```
        *Ben spots a coconut juice vendor and buys a cup.*
        *Mateo wanders over too. Damien stays put in the middle of the road, waiting.*
        *Mateo buys his own cup, then holds it out toward Damien.*
        Mateo: You're not getting one?
        *Damien glances at a coconut tree nearby, half-smiling.*
        Damien: If I wanted one, I'd have just made mysel—
        *Mateo cuts him off, already offering half the cup.*
        *Damien takes it. Drinks.*
    ```

## Outcome
* The group is locked at four for Saturday; Ben's absence from the next scene is now accounted for.
* Damien lets a small, unearned kindness happen (the shared juice) without deflecting it — a quiet contrast to
how hard he worked to deflect the guitar question minutes earlier.

# Scene: Going to the Old House

## Context
* **Location:** Road near the school → an old, narrow residential pathway
* **Setting:** Saturday morning
* **Characters:** Damien, Mateo, Elena (Joan running late, arrives off-page/via message)

## Objective
* Use Mateo and Elena's outsider awe at the hero-ecosystem backdrop to underline how normalized — and numbing
— it is for Damien.
* Get Damien to say something real (his grandparents, the empty house) and then let him retreat from that sincerity the
same way he retreated from the guitar question — through deflection, this time staged as a prank instead of a joke.
* End on a cliffhanger that lands as genuinely frightening — Mateo and Elena rattled for real, and Damien himself
selling the fear hard enough that the reader has no reason to doubt him yet. The reveal next chapter should land
as *"he committed to that bit way harder than made sense,"* not as a soft gotcha.

## Key Beats
* Damien meets Mateo and Elena at the roadside, checking his watch.
    ```
        Damien: Where's Joan?
        Elena: Not replying. Might still be asleep.
        Damien: Is her house far? I can just fly us there and send her the location after.
        Damien: Fine.
        *Damien forms a pink field around them and lifts off.*
        *Elena grins, delighted. Mateo looks confused — Damien's heading the opposite direction from his usual
        after-school route.*
        Mateo: (pointing) Dude, pretty sure your house is that way.
        Damien: Yeah. I told you — we're going to my old house.
    ```
* Mid-flight, they pass glimpses of the hero ecosystem: heroes resting between calls, a couple fighting off a
low-tier monster, a parent drilling their kid on flight basics.
* Damien watches all of it with visible boredom. Mateo and Elena are openly amazed.
    ```
        Elena: Is it always like this?
        Damien: Always has been.
        Mateo: When'd you start doing this? The flying thing.
        Damien: Can't remember exactly — maybe fourteen. Technically it's not flying, though. I'm floating —
        I build an invisible platform and push us with it.
        Mateo: Like a Lime Lantern?
        Damien: No. Lime Lantern flies without a platform under him.
        Damien: Also — I don't love being compared to superheroes.
        *Mateo doesn't push it. A quiet stretch follows.*
    ```
* They land in a narrow, mossy pathway among old houses.
    ```
        Damien: Here we are.
        *Elena scans for a house but only finds an old school building, a few shuttered stores, birds picking at
        the pavement.*
        Damien: My bad — I'm not that familiar with it anymore. Been a while.
        Damien: ...Oh. There it is.
        *This way.*
        *A middle-aged neighbor hanging laundry spots him and waves.*
        Neighbor: Hey, Damien! You with your mom?
        Damien: No — just staying a while. School project.
        *She smiles and goes back to the laundry.*
        Damien: That was my mom's friend.
    ```
* A small fake-out gag before the real house: Damien points them at a red-roofed house first.
    ```
        Damien: Oh — here it is.
        *Elena reaches to knock. Damien stops her.*
        Damien: Kidding.
        *Mateo laughs at Elena and keeps walking. Mateo gets close to the actual next door — a dog barks and
        lunges at the fence, startling him. Damien and Elena laugh.*
        Damien: Okay, okay — for real this time. This one's mine.
    ```
* The real house: unfinished two-tone paint job (half gray, half bright yellow), Christmas lights strung outside
year-round.
    ```
        Mateo: Your house is celebrating Christmas a little early, huh.
        Damien: We never really take those down. They just stay up.
        *Damien lets them in. Two small rooms, a round dining table in the middle. He sweeps a bit of dust off
        the floor with his foot.*
        Damien: Sit wherever. Send Joan the pin so she can catch up.
        *Elena starts typing.*
        Elena (in chat): Joan, it's already 10AM, are you still asleep?
        Mateo: This place is cozy. Why's nobody living here?
        Damien: It was my grandparents' place. They died four years ago — from a disease—
    ```
* **The prank (escalated):** Mid-sentence, Damien's hand drifts to the old switch panel by the door. The overhead
light stutters — once, then again, longer this time, like it's struggling to hold. A beat of near-dark. In it, a
chair near Mateo scrapes back against the floor on its own (Damien nudges it with his foot, unseen), and something
in the second room knocks against a wall. Mateo and Elena go quiet in the specific way people go quiet when a joke
hasn't landed as a joke yet. Damien plays it completely straight — no wink, no tell, nothing for the reader to
catch. He lets the fear sit a second too long before he moves.
    ```
        *The overhead light flickers — once, then holds a beat too long in near-dark before stuttering back.*
        *A chair scrapes back on its own near Mateo. Something knocks against the wall in the next room.*
        *Elena's laugh dies in her throat. Mateo goes very still.*
        Mateo: (quiet) ...Damien?
        *Damien doesn't answer right away. He's looking toward the other room, jaw tight, like he's actually
        listening for something.*
        Damien: No. I shouldn't have brought you here. Outside, now!
    ```
    * He herds them toward the door without waiting for questions — not rough, but fast, the kind of urgency
    that doesn't leave room to argue. The chapter ends before anyone's outside.

## Outcome
* Ends the chapter on a genuine cliffhanger — Mateo and Elena scared for real, Damien's urgency selling it
completely, with nothing in the prose that tips the reader off early.
* Sets up next chapter's reveal: it was Damien the whole time — the switch panel, the nudged chair, the noise
from the other room — no powers involved, just how far he was willing to go to avoid finishing the sentence
about his grandparents. The "outside, now" line will need to be recontextualized next chapter (panic that was
actually performance, urgency that was actually exit-strategy) — worth deciding then whether Damien felt guilty
about how far he took it, since scaring his friends for real is a heavier note than the "fine, I'll play guitar"
deflection from scene one.
