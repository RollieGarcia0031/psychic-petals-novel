# Chapter 5: Flower in The Cracks of Concrete

Damien finished lunch before the others.

He took his lunchbox outside and sat on the concrete step beside the house.

The road was busy.

Cars passed in both directions. Tricycles slowed near the corner. Motorcycles slipped between them, their engines
fading as they disappeared down the road.

Damien watched them.

He wasn't really thinking about anything.

He just liked watching things move.

Behind him, Joan and Elena were still talking.

He could hear them through the open door.

Inside the house, Elena laughed.

Damien heard his own name.

He kept looking at the road.

Joan's voice followed.

"Well, I can't see any sign here that she hates Damien for what happened last time."

Damien fed the dog another piece.

Elena said something he couldn't hear.

"But what if she's on a different social media account because she knows we know Damien?" Elena asked.

Then Joan answered.

"She's probably going to tell me that."

"How are you sure?"

"Because on the first day of school, out of nowhere, she was already asking me the name of the boy sitting in the
front row."

Damien stopped moving.

The dog waited.

"Really?", Elena asked.

Joan drank a glass of water and said "Yeah. I didn't know Damien yet that day."

"So you were close friends?"

There was a pause.

"Uhm. We're in a band, actually."

Elena laughed.

"Well, Damien is so lucky to have her."

Damien looked at the road.

A tricycle passed.

Then a jeepney.

Then a motorcycle with a sack of vegetables tied behind the seat.

He gave the dog the last piece of meat.

The dog licked his fingers before walking back toward the shade.

Damien wiped his hand on his pants.

He had not heard anything useful.

That was what he told himself.

Inside, Mateo suddenly shouted.

"Guys."

Damien looked through the open doorway.

Mateo was staring at his phone.

"What?"

"Ms. Shiela sent something."

Ben looked over.

"Read it."

Mateo cleared his throat.

"'Good morning, class. We will be having a performance task for finals. You have to sing a song about physics. You
can pick any formula, theorem, or law, just make sure to provide thorough explanation and examples.'"

He scrolled.

"'I'm giving you the freedom to pick your own groupmates, however each group must only have four members.'"

Silence.

Mateo lowered the phone.

"Seriously?"

Joan laughed.

"I don't think she likes us."

"This is a waste of time."

"You haven't even started."

"I thought we'd be doing experiments."

"Like what?"

"Like those professors on YouTube."

Mateo waved his spoon around.

"Wrecking balls. Falling objects. Explosions."

Ben took the spoon away from him, and wiped the table with a gray cloth.

"It's almost one."

Mateo looked at the clock.

"So?"

"We should go."

They finished lunch.

Damien stayed outside until the others came out.

Joan locked the door.

---

The next period was mathematics.

Sir Kent wrote three words across the board.

**FUNCTIONS.**

**EQUATIONS.**

**RELATIONSHIPS.**

He turned around.

"I'm pretty sure you're familiar with our first lesson."

Someone groaned.

Sir Kent smiled.

"This lesson has been the first lesson of every school year for as long as I've been teaching."

He began explaining.

Functions.

Inputs and outputs.

Equations.

Relationships between quantities.

Damien copied the examples into his notebook.

The room was warm.

The electric fan pushed air from one side of the classroom to the other without making it any cooler.

Sir Kent capped his marker.

"Now, talking about..."

"Relationships." said with a joking smile.

A few students laughed already.

"Who here has already had one, or currently has one?"

Hands went up.

Almost all of them.

Damien kept his down.

Sir Kent looked around.

"Okay. The rest of you are either very young, very honest, or very good at hiding things."

The class laughed.

Damien looked toward the window.

Then, without meaning to, he looked back.

Dahlia was looking at him.

They held each other's eyes for a moment.

He looks at her hand raising.

She saw his hands, kept in his lap.

Damien looked away.

Sir Kent laughed.

"There's nothing to be embarrassed about."

He turned toward the board.

Then stopped.

The room quieted a little.

"You know, before I met my wife, I was a completely different person."

Students then lowered their hands.

Sir Kent continued.

"I enrolled in engineering in college, but dropped out in my second year."

Damien's pen stopped.

"I got involved in some crime, and I was too afraid to tell my parents."

The room had become very quiet.

"So I dropped out. But a friend helped me finish studying. Different course. Different university."

He smiled.

"That friend is my wife now."

He laughed loudly.

"God, I'm lucky I met someone so patient with me."

"And she's rich by the way"

A few students laughed with him.

Sir Kent turned back to the board.

"Anyways. Back to the topic."

The marker squeaked.

Damien looked at his notebook.

He had stopped writing what he sees on the white board.

He looked at the teacher.

Then at Dahlia.

Then down again.

*There were things a person could keep hidden for years.*

*Apparently, there were also things a person could say in the middle of a math lesson.*

He started writing again.

---

After school, Damien walked with Mateo and Ben.

The road was crowded with students.

Motorcycles moved between jeepneys. Tricycles waited beside the stores. Vendors called out from beneath umbrellas
while the afternoon heat gathered on the pavement.

A couple walked ahead of them.

The girl held the boy's arm.

Mateo noticed Damien looking.

"Dude."

Damien looked at him.

"What?"

"It's obvious she likes you."

"Who?"

Mateo stared at him.

"Seriously?"

Ben laughed.

"He's pretending."

"I'm not."

"You are."

Damien kept walking.

Mateo moved closer.

"What are you even waiting for? The end of the world?"

Damien said nothing.

Ben looked at him.

"He's kind of right."

Damien glanced at him.

"Don't encourage him."

Ben smiled.

"Don't you feel anything for her?"

Damien kept walking.

"Even a little?"

The road narrowed.

Their footsteps fell into the same rhythm.

Damien could have said no.

It would have been easy.

Instead, he said,

"I think I can..."

Mateo and Ben slowed down.

Damien heard their footsteps become quieter behind him.

He knew exactly what they were waiting for.

He could have finished the sentence.

He didn't.

He looked toward the road ahead.

A motorcycle passed.

A woman carrying groceries crossed between two parked tricycles.

Someone was playing music from a store.

Damien took another step.

"...I guess I can play."

Mateo blinked.

"Play what?"

"For Ms. Shiela's performance task."

Ben frowned.

"The song?"

"The song, dude."

Mateo stared at him for a second.

Then he laughed.

"Oh. Right."

He scratched his head.

"We forgot to ask when that's even due."

Damien smiled faintly.

He had chosen the exit.

It was better than answering.

He kept walking.

*I'm not stupid enough to not understand what they're talking about.*

*But the truth is, the way I see her is like a flower that somehow grew between the cracks in concrete.*

*It's not supposed to happen.*

*But it does.*

*I wish I had the soil for it to grow.*

*But how?*

Damien kept walking.

He didn't notice when Mateo turned onto another street.

He didn't notice when Ben said goodbye.

He didn't notice that their footsteps had disappeared.

He just kept walking.

By the time he looked up, he was already standing in front of his house.

Damien stopped.

He looked behind him.

The road was empty.

He had walked the whole way home.

He hadn't flown once.
