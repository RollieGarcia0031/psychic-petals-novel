# Chapter 6: The Lights We Keep

The next morning, Mateo was already lying across Damien's desk before the first bell.

Damien stopped beside his chair.

Mateo looked up from his phone.

"Hey guys, Damien just told me yesterday he can play for us in the task!"

Elena looked away from her phone.

"Wow, so you can play instruments?"

"An instrument," Damien corrected.

He put his bag beneath the desk.

Then he remembered what he had said.

"Wait, no. I was just messing with Mateo yesterday."

Joan looked up from her phone.

"Lemme guess. Violin?"

"Guitar."

Damien sat down.

"But still, no. I can't. I'm just a beginner."

Joan smiled.

"Oh, come on. Everyone has a beginner's phase."

Elena nodded.

"Besides, we can pick easier songs for you to play."

Damien opened his notebook.

"Or you could just save an mp3 instrumental. Problem solved, right?"

Ben was checking his braces in a small pocket mirror.

"Live performance will probably sound better, though."

Mateo pushed himself up on one elbow.

"Besides..."

He looked across the room.

Dahlia was talking with two classmates near the windows, her guitar case resting beside her chair.

Mateo looked back at Damien.

"Girls like that, you know—"

He stopped.

Damien looked at him.

He didn't smile.

He didn't frown.

He just looked.

Mateo held the stare for a second.

Then another.

His grin disappeared.

He looked away first and cleared his throat.

"—anyway. So. You in or not?"

Damien exhaled through his nose.

He looked at the notebook in front of him.

Then at the guitar diagrams he had copied the night before.

Three chords.

One broken string.

A lot of sore fingers.

*As if I can stop these people.*

"Fine."

Mateo sat upright.

"Yes!"

"But I get to pick the song."

Joan raised both hands.

"Deal."

Elena smiled.

"Deal."

Ben put away his mirror.

"Works for me."

Mateo pointed at Damien.

"See? Easy."

Damien looked at his fingertips.

The skin was still red around the places where the strings had pressed into them.

*Jeez. I hope they don't pick something with barre chords.*

The bell rang.

Everyone returned to their seats.

Ms. Shiela entered a few minutes later with a folder under one arm.

She placed it on the desk.

"Good morning."

The class answered.

"Good morning, ma'am."

She opened the folder.

"Before we continue Newton's laws, I want everyone to remember that your physics performance task is still due."

Mateo slowly lowered his head onto the desk.

Ms. Shiela looked at him.

"Yes, Mateo. You are allowed to suffer quietly."

A few students laughed.

She turned toward the board.

"Newton's laws. The apple guy, as you already know."

Damien looked down at his notes.

His left hand rested beside the notebook.

Joan, seated across the room, noticed the red marks on his fingertips.

She leaned slightly toward him.

"That's gonna hurt for a few weeks."

Damien looked at her.

"Great."

"Months, maybe."

"Even better."

"But worth it once the calluses set in."

Damien looked at his hand again.

He rubbed his thumb over the sore tips of his fingers.

Ms. Shiela continued writing.

"Force equals mass times acceleration."

The chalk squeaked.

Damien copied it.

Then Ms. Shiela looked toward the class.

"By the way, if I were you, I'd be taking more notes."

She pointed the chalk toward the rows.

"Gives you more to say in your song project."

Joan and Elena caught Damien's eye from across the room.

Joan quietly raised both fists.

Elena gave him a small thumbs-up.

Damien stared at them.

Then he looked back at his notebook.

The lesson continued.

At the end of class, Ms. Shiela closed her folder.

"After that project, you'll also start on a capstone project for the second quarter."

Mateo raised his hand.

"Excuse me, ma'am."

Ms. Shiela looked at him.

"Yes?"

"Do we really have to do that? I thought capstone stuff was for college."

Ms. Shiela smiled.

"Good thing you'll already have the experience, then."

The class laughed.

"Come college, you'll be ready."

Mateo slowly lowered his hand.

"That's terrifying."

Damien smiled faintly.

School was apparently preparing them to be prepared for the things that would prepare them for more things.

He wrote the next equation.

---

The final bell came after literature.

Sir Marco stood beside the door while everyone packed their bags.

"Alright, class. Goodbye."

"Goodbye, sir."

He raised one finger.

"And bring your own pad papers next time, because next week I'm not letting anyone borrow from classmates."

The room erupted.

"Oh, come on..."

Sir Marco laughed.

"Goodbye."

Most of the class filtered into the hallway.

Damien stayed at his desk, packing his notebook and pencil case.

Mateo, Elena, and Joan gathered around him.

Ben stood nearby.

Elena looked around.

"Guys, we forgot to count heads."

Damien looked up.

"Ms. Shiela said four per group, but we're five here."

She counted them with her fingers.

"So either someone's group is short, or—"

"I already got my own group."

Ben said it quietly.

Everyone looked at him.

Mateo blinked.

"Wait. Why didn't you tell us?"

Joan looked surprised.

"When did you even plan this?"

Elena frowned.

"Which group? Are you sure?"

Ben scratched the back of his head.

"Yeah. I'll go with Reynold's group."

He shrugged.

"I mean, Elena and Mateo can sing. Joan and Damien can play."

He looked down.

"I'm tone-deaf. I don't want to be the reason it falls apart."

Damien wasn't surprised.

He closed his bag.

Mateo leaned against the desk.

"It's a physics project, man. Not a music subject. Nobody's grading us that hard."

Damien stood.

He walked toward the windows and began closing them one by one.

As he passed Ben, he gave his arm a light punch.

"You're too serious, dude."

Ben laughed.

"Sorry."

Joan waved a hand.

"It's fine. If Ben's already got plans with the other guys."

Ben smiled.

"See you guys."

"See you," Elena said.

Ben left.

The classroom became quieter.

Damien closed the last window.

Joan slung her bag over one shoulder.

"Anyway, we can't record at my place on weekends."

"Why?"

"My mom doesn't like a lot of people in the house."

Damien picked up his bag.

"We can go to my place."

Mateo froze.

Then he slowly turned toward the others.

"Oh-oh-oh."

Damien looked at him.

Mateo pointed dramatically.

"You heard him, guys."

He grinned.

"He said his place."

Damien rolled his eyes.

"I'm suggesting. Not requesting."

Elena nodded.

"I'm fine with Damien's place."

Joan smiled.

"Yeah, same."

Mateo clapped once.

"Okay. Case closed."

Damien opened the classroom door.

"You people decide things too quickly."

"That's leadership."

"That's annoying."

"Same thing."

They walked out.

---

The afternoon road was already crowded.

Students left through the school gate in groups, some walking, some riding motorcycles, others floating a few feet above
the pavement when they thought no teacher was watching.

Damien walked with Mateo and Ben.

They reached the corner.

Ben stopped.

"Wait."

He pointed toward a roadside vendor beneath a coconut tree.

"Coconut juice."

Mateo immediately followed.

Damien stayed in the middle of the road, waiting.

Ben bought a cup.

Mateo bought one too.

Damien watched the vendor open another coconut.

He glanced at the tree above them.

Mateo noticed.

"You're not getting one?"

Damien looked at the cup in Mateo's hand.

"If I wanted one, I'd have just made mysel—"

Mateo cut him off.

He held the cup toward Damien.

"Here."

Damien stared at it.

The juice was cold.

Water had collected around the plastic cup and ran over Mateo's fingers.

Damien took it.

He drank.

The coconut tasted slightly sweet and faintly smoky from the stall's ice.

Mateo smiled.

"See?"

Damien handed the cup back.

"Thanks."

Mateo took another drink.

Ben looked at them.

"You guys are weird."

Damien nodded.

"Yeah."

They continued walking.

For a while, nobody said anything.

The road carried them home.

---

Saturday morning was warmer than Damien expected.

He stood beside the roadside checking his watch.

9:47.

He looked down the road.

Nothing.

He checked his phone.

No new messages.

A motorcycle passed.

Then another.

Finally, Elena came walking toward him with Mateo beside her.

Damien lowered his phone.

"Where's Joan?"

Elena looked at her own phone.

"Not replying."

Mateo shrugged.

"Might still be asleep."

Damien looked at the time.

"It's almost ten."

"She's Joan."

"Fair."

Damien looked at the road behind them.

"Is your house far?"

Elena frowned.

"My house?"

"Yeah."

He pointed toward the sky.

"I can just fly us there and send her the location after."

Mateo looked at him.

"Wait, we're not going to your house?"

Damien looked at him.

"My old house."

Mateo blinked.

"What's the difference?"

Damien sighed.

"Fine."

He raised one hand.

A pale pink field spread beneath them.

It was smooth and almost transparent, catching the morning light along its edges.

Their feet left the pavement.

Elena immediately grinned.

Mateo grabbed the edge of his shirt.

Damien pushed them forward.

The houses became smaller.

The road stretched beneath them.

They passed a row of people standing on the roofs of a building, watching a licensed hero land nearby.

A few streets later, two heroes were fighting a low-tier monster beside an unfinished apartment building.

One hero struck it with a glowing metal staff.

The monster rolled through a parked tricycle.

Nobody seemed particularly surprised.

A man across the street pulled his child closer.

The child was already wearing a small flight harness.

The father held both hands behind the boy's back.

"Again."

The boy jumped.

He floated.

Only for a second.

Then he dropped.

The father caught him.

"Again."

Elena looked down.

"Is it always like this?"

Damien barely glanced at the scene.

"Always has been."

Mateo pointed toward the road.

"When'd you start doing this?"

"Can't remember exactly."

Damien looked ahead.

"Maybe fourteen."

He shifted the pink field beneath them.

"Technically it's not flying, though."

Mateo frowned.

"What?"

"I'm floating."

Damien rested one hand against the edge of the field.

"I build an invisible platform and push us with it."

Mateo thought about that.

"Like a Lime Lantern?"

Damien looked at him.

"No."

He looked back toward the road.

"Lime Lantern flies without a platform under him."

A short silence followed.

Damien added,

"Also, I don't love being compared to superheroes."

Mateo nodded.

"Okay."

Nobody said anything for a while.

The wind moved through their clothes.

The city thinned.

The roads became narrower.

The buildings became older.

Damien looked down.

The neighborhood below was one he hadn't visited properly in years.

He lowered the field.

They landed in a narrow residential pathway.

Moss had grown along the edges of the concrete.

An old school building stood on one side.

Several small stores were closed behind metal shutters.

Birds picked at crumbs between cracks in the pavement.

Elena looked around.

"Where's the house?"

Damien looked left.

Then right.

He frowned.

"My bad."

He scratched his head.

"I'm not that familiar with it anymore."

He walked forward.

"Been a while."

They passed another row of houses.

Damien stopped.

"...Oh."

He pointed.

"There it is."

A middle-aged woman was hanging clothes outside one of the houses.

She looked up.

Her face changed.

"Hey, Damien!"

Damien waved.

"Hi."

She looked behind him.

"You with your mom?"

"No."

He pointed toward Mateo and Elena.

"Just staying a while. School project."

The woman smiled.

"Ah."

She returned to the laundry.

"Don't make too much noise."

Damien smiled.

"We won't."

They walked away.

Mateo looked at him.

"Who was that?"

"My mom's friend."

"She remembers you."

"Yeah."

Damien kept walking.

They reached a red-roofed house.

Damien stopped.

"Oh."

He pointed at it.

"Here it is."

Elena stepped forward.

She raised her hand toward the door.

Damien caught her wrist.

"Kidding."

Elena turned.

"What?"

Mateo started laughing.

"Bro."

He pointed at her.

"You almost knocked."

Elena glared at Damien.

"That's not funny."

Damien smiled.

"It was a little funny."

Mateo walked past them.

"Come on."

He reached the next house.

A dog suddenly barked behind the fence.

It lunged against the metal bars.

Mateo jumped backward.

"Jesus!"

Damien laughed.

Elena laughed too.

Mateo looked at both of them.

"You two are terrible."

Damien wiped his mouth.

"Okay, okay."

He pointed toward the house beside them.

"For real this time."

He looked at the door.

"This one's mine."

---

The house had been painted twice and never properly finished.

One side was gray.

The other was bright yellow.

The colors met in an uneven line near the front window.

Christmas lights hung along the roof.

They were still there.

Mateo stared upward.

"Your house is celebrating Christmas a little early, huh."

Damien looked at the lights.

"We never really take those down."

He pushed the gate open.

"They just stay up."

He unlocked the door.

The hinges complained when he pulled it open.

Inside were two small rooms divided by a narrow passage.

A round dining table sat in the middle.

Dust had settled over the surface.

A wooden chair stood slightly away from the table.

Damien walked in first.

He swept some dust from the floor with the side of his shoe.

"Sit wherever."

He put his bag down.

"Send Joan the pin so she can catch up."

Elena took out her phone.

She opened the group chat.

**Elena:** Joan, it's already 10AM, are you still asleep?

No reply.

Mateo looked around.

"This place is cozy."

He touched the back of one chair.

"Why's nobody living here?"

Damien looked at the table.

His fingers rested against the edge.

"It was my grandparents' place."

Mateo waited.

Damien looked toward the second room.

"They died four years ago."

He paused.

"From a disease—"

His hand moved toward the old switch panel beside the door.

The room was warm.

Dust floated through the narrow strip of sunlight coming through the window.

Damien touched the switch.

The overhead light flickered.

Once.

Then again.

This time it stayed dark a little longer.

Mateo stopped moving.

Elena lowered her phone.

The light returned.

A chair scraped across the floor beside Mateo.

He turned.

The chair had moved.

Something knocked against the wall in the second room.

Elena's mouth opened.

No sound came out.

Mateo stared toward the hallway.

The house had become very quiet.

Inside, nobody moved.

Mateo swallowed.

"...Damien?"

Damien didn't answer immediately.

He looked toward the second room.

His jaw tightened.

He listened.

Another small knock came from somewhere behind the wall.

Damien took one step backward.

"No."

He looked at Mateo.

Then Elena.

"I shouldn't have brought you here."

He reached for the door.

"Outside, now!"
