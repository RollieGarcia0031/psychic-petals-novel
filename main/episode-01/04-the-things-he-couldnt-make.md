# Chapter 4: The Things He Couldn't Make

The alarm rang at six in the morning.

Damien opened one eye.

Something heavy moved outside.

He listened.

A distant crash rolled through the neighborhood, followed by a sound like metal being dragged across concrete.

Damien turned off the alarm.

For a few seconds, he stayed in bed.

Another crash came from somewhere beyond the window.

Then his mother spoke inside his head.

*“Damien.”*

He sat up.

*“Yeah?”*

*“Don't go outside yet.”*

Damien looked toward the bedroom door.

*“Why?”*

There was a pause.

*“There's something outside.”*

He stood and walked to the window.

The curtains were already shaking.

Beyond the houses, something enormous rose above the trees.

It had too many tentacles.

Its mouth was wider than a jeepney.

Several long teeth hung from its upper jaw.

Damien stared at it.

The creature moved forward.

A house disappeared beneath one of its tentacles.

Damien sighed.

*“Great.”*

His mother spoke again.

*“I haven't cooked anything.”*

Damien looked back toward the bed.

His mother was still lying there, one hand pressed against her temple.

*“My head hurts.”*

*“You should sleep.”*

*“I know.”*

She closed her eyes.

*“There's bread downstairs. Just buy something for breakfast.”*

Damien changed his shirt.

He put on his slippers and walked downstairs.

Outside, people were already running toward the other side of the road.

A woman carried a child.

A man pulled his motorcycle out of a garage.

Someone shouted that the monster was coming closer.

Damien stepped outside.

He looked at the sky.

The creature was moving slowly.

A translucent pink wall appeared around the neighborhood, stretching from one end of the street to the other and curving over the houses like a dome.

The next tentacle struck it.

The impact shook the windows.

Damien watched the shield ripple.

He yawned.

*“Yes.”*

He looked toward the bakery.

*“No classes today.”*

---

The baker was already standing outside.

He had a broom in one hand and a paper bag in the other.

*“Morning,”* the baker said.

*“Morning,”* Damien replied.

Damien pointed at the bread.

*“One of those, please.”*

The baker handed him the bag.

Another crash shook the road.

The baker looked toward the sky.

*“Monsters appearing during the first week of school.”*

He sighed.

*“Students must be celebrating.”*

Damien opened the paper bag.

*“I am.”*

The baker laughed.

*“At least you won't have Friday quizzes.”*

Damien looked at him.

*“It's all fun and games until the teacher says, ‘We don't have plenty of time because of the suspension, so you need to advance study these chapters.’”*

The baker laughed harder.

Damien smiled.

Above them, something bright moved across the sky.

A man in a blue suit flew directly toward the monster.

A sword appeared in his hand.

Damien watched him.

*“Wonder Man.”*

The hero swung the sword.

It struck one of the creature's tentacles.

The tentacle barely moved.

Wonder Man flew backward.

Another figure arrived from the opposite direction.

Super Woman.

She caught the falling hero before he hit the ground.

Damien took a bite of bread.

More heroes appeared.

One wore green armor shaped vaguely like a vegetable.

Another had a bright orange costume and a cape that was far too short.

Damien watched for another few seconds.

Then he went home.

---

The television was already reporting the attack when Damien returned.

He put the bread on the table.

His mother was still upstairs.

Damien washed rice.

He poured it into a pot, added water, and put it on the stove.

The rice would cook faster this way.

He glanced through the window.

The monster was still there.

Wonder Man had disappeared beneath one of its tentacles.

Super Woman was carrying three civilians away from a collapsing building.

Vegetaman fired a green beam from both hands.

KarrotBoy arrived a few seconds later.

Damien stared at him.

*“That guy again.”*

KarrotBoy raised both hands.

A bright sphere formed between them.

Damien lowered the heat.

The sphere exploded against the monster.

The whole street flashed white.

Damien looked at the stove.

The rice had started boiling.

He adjusted the flame.

His mother finally came downstairs.

She was still holding her head.

*“You're cooking?”* she asked.

*“Rice.”*

*“I told you to buy something.”*

*“I did.”*

He pointed at the bread.

His mother looked at it.

*“Good.”*

She sat down.

Damien checked the stove again.

Then his phone vibrated.

He picked it up.

The class group chat was full of messages.

**Dahlia:** Seriously?

A second message appeared.

**Dahlia:** Our class is 7AM.

**Dahlia:** And the suspension was announced at 6:49?

Elena replied.

**Elena:** Don't tell me you're at school right now.

Another message appeared.

**Dahlia:** I am.

Damien stared at the screen.

Then he looked at the stove.

His mother noticed.

*“What is it?”* she asked.

*“Nothing.”*

He lowered the flame and placed the lid over the pot.

*“I'll be back.”*

His mother looked at him.

*“Where?”*

*“School.”*

*“School is suspended.”*

*“I know.”*

*“Then why are you going?”*

Damien was already walking toward the door.

*“I forgot something.”*

His mother sighed.

*“Damien.”*

He stopped.

*“Don't do anything stupid.”*

He opened the door.

*“I won't.”*

He paused.

*“Probably.”*

---

The school was almost empty.

The hallway felt different without students.

The electric fans were still running inside the classrooms, turning slowly beneath the ceiling.

Damien walked through the corridor.

He checked their classroom.

Empty.

He looked through the windows.

Nothing.

He turned around.

He was about to leave when someone spoke behind him.

*“You came too?”*

Damien stopped.

Dahlia stood at the end of the hallway.

She was still wearing her school uniform.

Her guitar wasn't with her.

She looked tired.

Damien looked at her.

*“Yeah.”*

She walked closer.

*“The mayor's really slow at announcing things.”*

*“I noticed.”*

She smiled.

*“How did you get here?”*

*“I live nearby.”*

*“You walked?”*

*“No.”*

She looked at his feet.

He was still wearing slippers.

Then she looked back at his face.

*“You flew.”*

Damien shrugged.

*“Maybe.”*

Dahlia smiled again.

*“You really don't care about hiding it, do you?”*

*“There's nobody here.”*

*“There was a whole monster outside.”*

*“Yeah.”*

*“And you're just walking around.”*

*“Yeah.”*

Dahlia laughed.

*“You are weird.”*

*“I've heard that.”*

They walked toward the school entrance.

A loud impact came from the distance.

Both of them looked up.

A piece of the monster's body had broken apart.

It fell through the sky.

Damien raised one hand.

A pink wall appeared above them.

The falling mass struck it.

The shield bent.

Then the piece slid sideways and crashed into an empty field.

Dahlia stared at him.

Damien lowered his hand.

*“That was close.”*

She looked at the destroyed field.

*“Fighting monsters looks like a piece of cake to you.”*

*“Not really.”*

*“You didn't even move.”*

*“I moved my hand.”*

*“That's not what I mean.”*

Damien looked at her.

She smiled.

*“You were watching the heroes fight while wearing pajamas earlier, weren't you?”*

*“How did you know?”*

*“You're still wearing them.”*

Damien looked down.

He had forgotten.

*“Oh.”*

Dahlia laughed.

*“Why are you so calm?”*

Damien thought about it.

*“I haven't eaten breakfast.”*

Dahlia stopped laughing.

*“What?”*

*“I'm cooking rice.”*

*“You're cooking rice?”*

*“Yeah.”*

*“While there's a monster outside?”*

*“The monster isn't in my kitchen.”*

Dahlia stared at him.

Then she laughed again.

*“I should go home.”*

*“Why?”*

*“I haven't finished my biology notes.”*

*“During a monster attack?”*

*“I have a test.”*

Damien nodded.

*“Fair.”*

She started walking toward the road.

Damien followed for a few steps.

Then he stopped.

*“Dahlia.”*

She turned.

*“Yeah?”*

He almost asked why she came to school.

Instead, he said:

*“Your class is suspended.”*

She smiled.

*“I know.”*

*“Then don't come tomorrow.”*

*“I'll try.”*

She walked away.

Damien watched her until she reached her motorcycle.

She put on her helmet.

The engine started.

Then she disappeared down the road.

Damien looked at the sky.

KarrotBoy raised both hands.

A bright sphere formed above him.

Damien recognized the move.

*“Spiritual Bomb.”*

The sphere fell.

The monster disappeared behind a wall of light.

Damien turned around.

He flew home.

---

His mother was standing in the kitchen when he returned.

She was turning off the stove.

*“You left it.”*

*“I lowered the heat.”*

*“You still left it.”*

Damien looked at the rice.

It was fine.

His mother put the lid aside.

*“I told you that when you're cooking, you cook.”*

*“I know.”*

*“Don't get distracted.”*

*“I wasn't distracted.”*

His mother looked at him.

Damien looked away.

She smiled.

*“You went to school.”*

*“Yeah.”*

*“Why?”*

Damien opened the paper bag.

He took out a piece of bread.

*“Dahlia was there.”*

His mother raised an eyebrow.

Damien took a bite.

*“I see.”*

Damien nodded.

His mother kissed his forehead.

*“Next time, don't leave the kitchen with an open stove.”*

*“I didn't.”*

*“You left it with the heat on.”*

*“Low heat.”*

*“Still.”*

Damien ate the bread.

Outside, the television announced that the monster had been defeated.

Damien wasn't listening anymore.

He was thinking about a girl standing in an empty school hallway.

---

The next morning, Damien stayed in his room.

His father was already eating breakfast downstairs.

Damien could smell fried eggs.

He could hear the television.

He didn't go down.

The guitar was underneath his bed.

He looked at it.

Then at the clock.

His father's car would leave soon.

He waited.

A spoon touched a plate downstairs.

A chair moved.

The front door opened.

His father's footsteps crossed the porch.

The car engine started.

Damien waited until the sound disappeared down the road.

Then he pulled the guitar from beneath his bed.

He sat on the floor.

The case opened with a dry click.

Inside was the same guitar he had found in the attic.

His father had bought it years ago.

He had never learned to play.

He had simply bought one because, at some point in his life, he had wanted to.

Then he stopped trying.

Damien didn't know why.

He only knew that the guitar had spent years in the attic without anyone touching it.

He lifted it.

The wood felt cold.

He opened the guitar case again.

A thin book was tucked beneath the neck.

**Guitar for Dummies.**

Damien picked it up.

He opened the first page.

The drawings looked simple.

A hand.

A guitar.

Strings.

Letters.

Numbers.

He stared at the diagrams.

Then he turned the page.

More diagrams.

He turned another.

More.

Damien frowned.

He looked at the guitar.

Then at the book.

*“Okay.”*

He placed the guitar against his leg.

He tried copying the hand position.

His fingers didn't fit.

He moved them.

Still wrong.

He looked at the picture.

Then at his hand.

Then back at the picture.

*“Why?”*

He tried again.

The guitar produced a sound that was neither a chord nor a note.

It sounded like someone dropping a metal spoon into a bucket.

Damien stared at it.

*“That's terrible.”*

He tried again.

It was worse.

He closed the book.

He reached for his laptop.

Tutorials were easier.

Probably.

---

Three hours later, they were not easier.

Damien watched one video.

Then another.

Then another.

He paused the screen.

He rewound it.

He paused again.

The instructor moved his fingers across the guitar.

Damien copied him.

The instructor changed chords.

Damien copied him.

The instructor smiled.

Damien looked at his own fingers.

They were nowhere near the right positions.

He closed the laptop.

He looked at the wall.

Then an idea came to him.

The wall became white.

Six thin horizontal lines appeared across it.

Then six more.

A large guitar neck formed between them.

Beside each chord, Damien wrote the numbers for the fingers.

The letters appeared underneath.

He stepped back.

The entire wall had become a practice sheet.

He looked at the guitar.

Then the wall.

Then the guitar again.

*“Much better.”*

He sat.

The first chord took him several minutes.

His fingers slipped.

He tried again.

The string buzzed.

Again.

Another string stopped ringing.

Again.

He finally produced something that sounded almost like a chord.

Damien looked at the wall.

*“One.”*

He changed his fingers.

The second chord.

Buzz.

Again.

Buzz.

Again.

The third.

Nothing.

He moved one finger.

The sound changed.

He smiled.

*“Okay.”*

He continued.

The afternoon moved past the window.

The neighborhood became quieter.

The wall filled with notes.

The guitar strings became warm beneath his fingers.

His fingertips became red.

He kept playing.

---

By evening, Damien could play three chords badly.

He sat on his bed with the guitar across his lap.

His fingers hurt.

He looked at them.

There were small red marks across the tips.

He pressed one against the guitar string.

Pain.

He pulled it away.

Then he tried again.

His phone vibrated.

He looked at it.

A message from Ben.

**Ben:** School tomorrow?

Damien typed.

**Damien:** Probably.

Another message appeared.

**Ben:** You practicing something?

Damien stared at the guitar.

He typed:

**Damien:** No.

He deleted it.

Then typed:

**Damien:** Maybe.

Ben replied with a thumbs-up.

Damien put the phone down.

He looked at the guitar.

For some reason, he didn't want anyone to know.

Not yet.

---

Monday came with a quiz.

Damien sat at his desk.

The teacher walked between the rows, placing papers face down.

Damien watched the paper land in front of him.

He hadn't studied.

He had spent most of the weekend with a guitar.

He flipped the paper over.

True or false.

Damien stared at the first question.

Then the second.

Then the third.

He leaned back.

*“Good.”*

He picked up his pen.

*“Not cooked today.”*

The teacher looked toward him.

*“What did you say?”*

Damien straightened.

*“Nothing, Sir.”*

He answered the quiz.

He got lucky.

---

During recess, everyone went toward the canteen.

Damien didn't.

He walked toward the large acacia tree behind the building.

Something small moved beneath it.

A cat.

Then another.

Four kittens were pressed against their mother.

Damien sat on the ground.

The mother cat didn't move away.

One kitten climbed over another.

Another slept with its face buried against the mother's side.

Damien watched.

He didn't know how long he had been there when someone called his name.

*“Damien!”*

He didn't turn.

The kittens were more interesting.

*“Damien!”*

Nothing.

A third voice joined.

*“Damiennn!”*

He turned.

Dahlia stood several meters away.

Behind her were Elena, Mateo, and Joan.

Dahlia smiled.

Damien looked at her.

She smiled wider.

Then she winked.

Elena covered her mouth.

*“I'm pretty sure Damien's blushing.”*

*“I'm not.”* Damien said.

Mateo laughed.

*“You're definitely blushing.”*

Damien looked back at the kittens.

Dahlia's smile disappeared slightly.

She waited.

Damien didn't say anything.

Mateo looked between them.

*“Ouch.”*

Elena nudged him.

*“Don't.”*

*“What?”*

*“You're making it worse.”*

Dahlia looked down.

Then she smiled again, but it wasn't the same smile.

*“Anyway.”*

She turned.

*“Let's go.”*

The others followed.

Damien watched the kittens.

One of them had stopped feeding.

It climbed over its mother's leg and fell back into the grass.

Damien smiled.

He looked toward the path.

Dahlia was already gone.

He looked back at the kittens.

For some reason, he couldn't concentrate anymore.

---

That night, Damien couldn't sleep.

He lay on his back.

The ceiling fan turned slowly above him.

The room was dark.

His guitar rested against the wall.

He looked at it.

Then at his hands.

Then back at the ceiling.

He remembered Dahlia's face.

The smile.

The wink.

The moment her expression changed.

Damien turned onto his side.

He closed his eyes.

He opened them again.

He reached beneath his bed.

The guitar came out.

He sat on the edge of the mattress.

The house was quiet.

His parents were asleep.

Damien held the guitar carefully.

He placed his fingers on the first chord.

The string pressed against the sore tips of his fingers.

He winced.

He played it anyway.

The sound was quiet.

He changed chords.

The second one was worse.

He tried again.

Then the third.

The three chords came out unevenly, one after another.

Damien stopped.

He listened to the silence afterward.

Then he tried again.

The notes were still wrong.

He kept playing.

His fingers hurt.

He kept playing.

On the wall, the temporary guitar diagrams were still visible.

Six lines.

Three chords.

A road drawn beneath them.

Damien looked at the wall.

Then at the guitar.

He placed his fingers on the first chord again.

This time, he didn't think about whether it sounded good.

He just played.

And played.

Until the house was quiet enough that even the mistakes had somewhere to go.
