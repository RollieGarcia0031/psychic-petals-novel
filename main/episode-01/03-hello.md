# <center> Chapter 3 </center>

A word was written dry, on the chalkboard with a crumbling white chalk, and it was *HOME*.

Sir Marco leaned against the edge of his desk with his arms folded, his wristwatch ticking loudly against
the silence of the room. He had given them twenty minutes to produce a full essay, an unreasonable 
demand that had sent a wave of frantic scratching pens across the desks. 

Damien sat in his front-row corner, staring at his blank sheet of lined paper. The blue lines looked like
iron bars. He didn't want to write about the heavy, telepathic silence that hung over his mother’s soup
bowls, nor did he want to describe his father’s thick, tired shoulders at the dinner table. Instead, he
reached into his memory for fragments of late-night television broadcasts he had watched when sleep refused
to come—lines of dialogue spoken by actors in cheap costumes, stitched together until they resembled something
profound.

His pen moved across the page in swift, quiet strokes:

*It is strange why house and home are supposed to have different meanings. Home wasn't meant to be a place;
it's about the feeling that we know the people around us also care about us. But still, how can we consider
it home if it's a place where we have no air to breathe despite having our loved ones around us?*

He did not re-read what he had written. He simply capped his pen, stood up, and let the wooden legs of his
chair scrape against the linoleum. The sound cut through the frantic atmosphere of the classroom like a blade.

Sir Marco looked up over the rims of his reading glasses, his eyebrows lifting. "Finished already, Damien?"

Damien handed his paper over without a word. Sir Marco took it, scanned the crisp paragraphs, and let out
an appreciative hum. Then his eyes drifted toward the rest of the class, where thirty students were still
hunched over their desks, sweating under the humid afternoon air.

"Well," Sir Marco announced, tapping his knuckles against the podium. "If Damien can articulate his domestic
reflections in less than ten minutes, the rest of you shouldn't need twenty. You have ten seconds to turn
in whatever you've managed to write."

A collective gasp echoed through the room. Chairs shoved backward, papers rustled violently, and someone
in the middle row accidentally knocked a metal pencil case onto the floor with a loud clatter. 

"Ten," Sir Marco began, his voice calm and unyielding. "Nine. Eight..."

Damien
stood by the front desk, a sudden, cold prickle of guilt tightening in his chest. His quiet urge to get
the task over with had inadvertently pulled the floor out from under everyone else. He turned to walk back
to his desk when a sharp, muffled squeak caught his attention.

Dahlia was trapped. Her chair had caught the hem of her pleated skirt against the desk leg, pinning her
in place. She was leaning far over the wooden frame, her paper held out in midair, just out of reach of the aisle. 

She looked up at Damien. There was no panic in her eyes—only a bright, playful light as she sang out, her
voice loud enough to cut through the surrounding chaos: "Damie-Damie-Damien!!!"

Damien moved without thinking. He reached out and took the paper straight from her fingers. 

"Three," Sir Marco called out.

Students were scrambling into the narrow aisle, clutching their half-written pages like fluttering birds. 

Damien didn't take another step. The air inside the room grew dense, smelling faintly of ozone and crushed
stone. Before anyone could reach the front row, thirty translucent, pearlescent folders materialized midair
above the desks. They snapped shut around every loose sheet of paper in the room, catching airborne pages
and sliding across the room in smooth, silent trajectories straight onto Sir Marco's podium.

Damien, however, did not let Dahlia's paper fly. He held the single sheet between his thumb and forefinger,
walked the three steps to the front, and laid it gently on top of the glowing stack just as Sir Marco counted: "One."

A heavy silence dropped over the room. Classmates stood frozen in mid-stride, their hands still outstretched
toward the podium, staring at the neatly organized tower of pearlescent folders that was already fading
back into invisible air. Nobody had seen how it happened; it was faster than the sweep of an eye.

Dahlia, having finally untangled her skirt, leaned her chin on her hand, her dark eyes tracking Damien with a
slow, knowing smile. 

Damien slid back into his seat, pulled his drawing pad from his bag, and began sketching an unlabeled road, acting
as though he had been sitting there all along.

***

After the final bell, the sun turned the asphalt into a shimmering sheet of brass. Damien walked past the
school gates, keeping his eyes on the ground until he reached the edge of a nearby construction site. It
was a half-finished structure of raw concrete columns, rusted rebar jutting into the air, and piles of
dry cement bags—a quiet place where he could usually slip behind the shadows and take off into the sky unobserved.

He rounded a heavy concrete pillar, expecting empty dust, and stopped short.

Sitting on a stack of dry cement bags, sharing a crinkling bag of salty corn chips, were Joan, Elena, and
Mateo. 

They froze for a fraction of a second, chip bags held in midair, before Joan’s face split into a wide,
shameless grin. "We know who you are."

Damien stared at them, his expression flat and unreadable. "Yeah. Good for you."

Elena leaned forward, swinging her legs against the cement bags. "What kind of water do you drink at home
? How the heck did you get those powers?"

Damien adjusted the strap of his bag and said, his voice perfectly calm and devoid of heat: "Fuck you,
that's none of your business."

Joan let out a sharp bark of laughter, nearly choking on a corn chip. "Whoa! I wasn't expecting to hear
such crunchy curse words from the quiet front-row boy!"

"What?" Damien asked, tilting his head slightly. "You thought I was a shy type?"

Joan slapped her knee and stood up. "I like this dude! From now on, you're part of our friend group!"

Damien frowned, taking a half-step back. "What the hell? That's not how you make friends."

Mateo jumped down from the stack, dusting gray powder off his shorts. "Nah, dude, you're our friend from
now on! We'll call you Niemad! And you can call me Oetam!"

"That's not even a nickname," Damien muttered. "It's just our names backward."

"Yeah!" Joan declared cheerfully. "That's how we call each other in this group!"

Damien shook his head, looking at the three of them as if they had escaped from an asylum. "I didn't even
agree to your terms. You guys are insane. I'm getting hungry, whatever."

He stepped past the rebar into the open shadow behind the wall, his boots leaving the dust as he rose smoothly
into the afternoon air, flying toward the treeline before they could follow.

Behind him, Joan’s voice echoed over the concrete walls: "Ben! Your friend is sure weird—great addition
for stupid people like us!"

***

By the next morning, Damien’s quiet sanctuary in the front row was compromised. Mateo and Elena had shifted
their desks directly behind his, filling the quiet space behind his ears with a constant, low murmur of
gossip, passing folded notes over his shoulders and snickering at inside jokes.

Damien bore it for two periods before his patience wore thin. During literature class, he slowly turned
his head around and glared at them, raising a single finger to his lips in a silent, deadpan command to shut up.

Elena only grins. Instead of stopping, she reached out and casually began twirling a lock of Damien’s dark
hair around her finger. Damien stared at her for three seconds, realized the futility of resistance, and
slowly turned back to the blackboard, letting her play with his hair while he transcribed Sir Marco's notes.

When the lunch bell rang, Ben leaned over Damien’s desk. "Hey, Damien. We're all heading over to Joan's
place for lunch. Hop along."

They walked four blocks down a narrow alley to a small, low-slung house with faded green paint and a rusted
tin roof. Inside, the living room was quiet, cooled only by a squeaking ceiling fan. 

Ben looked around the empty hallway. "Where is everybody? I thought you had a sister?"

"My parents are at work, my sister’s at school," Joan said, tossing her bag onto a plastic chair.

"Damn, it must be boring around here," Ben remarked.

"Nah," Joan shrugged, walking into the kitchen. "We're busy at school anyway. Besides, at least I'm free
to do any stuff here."

Damien, sitting on the edge of a worn wooden stool, let a subtle, wicked smirk touch his lips. "Any stuff?"

Mateo and Elena burst into loud, sudden laughter, startled by the dark, sarcastic edge in Damien's voice.

They gathered around the small kitchen table. Joan brought out plates and spoons for Ben, Mateo, and Elena,
placing them next to a large pot of warm rice. Damien reached into his backpack and pulled out his own
metal container—a packed lunch his mother had prepared that morning.

"You sure you don't want a plate, Damien?" Joan offered, holding out a porcelain dish.

"I'm okay," Damien replied smoothly, popping the lid off his tin. "I brought my own."

He watched as Mateo picked up the serving spoon and scooped a mountain of white rice onto his plate without
an ounce of restraint or hesitation.

"Damn," Damien noted dryly. "You look sincerely hungry."

Mateo chewed a mouthful of rice and grinned back. "So are you, but not for food."

Damien blinked. "Poetic. Hmp."

Mateo set his spoon down and leaned over his plate. "So... can we ask you about your powers?"

Damien picked up his fork. "Uh... I guess."

Joan leaned her elbows on the plastic tablecloth. "We heard stories about you. There's actually a gossip
floating around that you used to get paid for beating up people back in middle school."

Damien didn't flinch. "Yeah, right. So what?"

"Why did you stop doing those things?" Joan asked, her eyes sharp with curiosity.

Damien stopped eating. He set his fork down with a quiet *clink*. "Who told you I stopped my plans?"

He held his hands open above the table. In a breath, the air around his palms shimmered with a cold, pearlescent
glow, solidifying into a heavy, razor-sharp hunting knife in his right hand and a massive iron pipe wrench
in his left. The metal looked dense, cold, and impossibly real.

Mateo and Elena gasped, pulling their chairs back.

Damien narrowed his eyes, letting them glow with a sudden, terrifying violet radiance, and roared in a
deep, booming villain voice: "SOON, I WILL TAKE OVER THIS WORLD!"

Elena put her hands up in genuine alarm: "Oh fuck, don't!"

Damien immediately dropped his hands. The knife and wrench dissolved into fine, pearlescent dust that vanished
before touching the tablecloth. He let out a quiet, satisfied sigh and smirked. "Damn. I really dreamed
of acting as the villain in a movie."

Mateo cracked up so violently that his spoon slipped from his fingers and clattered onto the floor next to Ben's foot. 

As the room filled with their loud, messy laughter, Damien picked his fork back up and took a quiet bite
of his lunch, a strange, unfamiliar warmth settling in his chest. 

*Damn,* he thought to himself, looking at his new, loud companions. *I shouldn't have rushed that essay...*
