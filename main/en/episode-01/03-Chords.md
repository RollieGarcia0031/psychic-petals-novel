# Chapter 3: The Sound Outside

The television was already playing when Damien sat down for breakfast.

A frying pan hissed beside him. His mother had left the kitchen window open, and the smell of fried egg mixed with the damp morning air coming in from the yard, along with the distant sound of motorcycles starting one after another along the road.

Damien broke the yolk with his spoon.

On the television, a man with a guitar sat beneath a row of studio lights.

The music was slow.

Damien stopped eating.

The man moved his fingers across the strings without looking at them. His left hand shifted along the neck while his right hand kept the rhythm almost lazily, each note falling into the next without any visible effort.

Eric Clapton.

Damien had heard the name before.

He didn't know much about him.

He watched anyway.

His father entered the dining room carrying a cup of coffee.

He sat across from Damien and glanced at the television.

"Eric Clapton."

Damien continued eating.

His father smiled.

"I used to listen to him all the time."

"Okay."

"When I was in high school, everyone was talking about him."

Damien scooped a piece of egg onto his rice.

His father continued.

"I wanted to play guitar too."

Damien looked up for a second.

His father noticed.

"I even had one."

"What happened?"

His father laughed.

"I stopped."

"Why?"

"Life."

Damien looked back at his plate.

His father drank his coffee.

"You know, music was different back then."

Damien didn't answer.

"You should hear some of his old songs."

"Maybe."

His father smiled.

"There are a lot of things you can learn from people who know what they're doing."

Damien pushed the rice around his plate.

On television, the guitarist's fingers moved again.

Damien watched them.

Then he looked down.

His breakfast had gone cold.

---
The first-period teacher didn't arrive.

By ten minutes, the class had stopped pretending they were waiting.

Joan sat beside Elena, painting her fingernails while Damien worked quietly at his desk. Mateo was drawing something on the back of his notebook. Ben was talking to two students near the window.

Then a guitar sounded from the hallway.

A few soft chords came through the open classroom door.

Damien looked up.

Dahlia was sitting on a bench outside with two of her friends, her guitar resting across her lap. She was singing quietly, the sound carrying into the classroom between the hum of the electric fan and the conversations around him.

Damien watched her.

More precisely, he watched her hands.

Her left hand moved along the neck of the guitar, pressing one string and then another. Her fingers changed positions quickly, almost without hesitation. Her right hand kept the rhythm with small, practiced movements.

He leaned forward slightly.

He tried to remember where each finger went.

Joan noticed.

"Damien."

He looked at her.

"What?"

She held up the nail polish bottle.

"You want some?"

Damien glanced at it.

"No."

"Why not?"

"I don't need it."

Joan laughed.

"Neither does Elena."

Elena raised her hand.

"Hey."

Joan ignored her and opened another bottle.

"This one's translucent. Nobody will probably notice."

Damien looked at the bottle.

Then he looked toward the hallway again.

Dahlia was still playing.

Joan followed his eyes.

Then she smiled.

"Oh."

Damien looked back at her.

"What?"

"Nothing."

She placed her hand near his.

"Would you mind?"

Damien stared at the brush.

The guitar continued outside.

He looked at his own left hand.

Then, after a moment, he placed it flat on the desk.

Joan's eyes widened.

"Oh, shit. Really?"

Damien shrugged.

"Just one."

Joan pulled his hand closer and began painting his fingernail.

The translucent polish caught the light for only a second before becoming almost invisible.

Damien barely looked at it.

His attention kept drifting toward the hallway.

Dahlia's fingers shifted again.

One chord.

Then another.

He memorized the movement.

Joan noticed him staring past her.

"Wow."

Damien looked down.

"What?"

"Your hands are softer than Elena's."

Elena frowned.

"Why are you comparing us?"

Joan kept painting.

"People with hands like these should play an instrument."

Damien glanced at his fingers.

"I don't."

"You should."

He looked toward the hallway again.

Dahlia was laughing with her friends now, one hand still resting on the guitar.

"Can you play?" Damien asked.

Joan shook her head.

"Violin. Not guitar."

"Oh."

Joan looked at him.

"Why?"

Damien turned his attention back to her.

"Nothing."

Outside, Dahlia began another song.

Damien listened.

Joan finished the nail and let go of his hand.

"There."

He looked at it.

The polish was barely there.

"Thanks."

Joan smiled.

Then she noticed where he was looking.

Dahlia's song continued through the doorway.

Joan leaned back in her chair.

"You really like that guitar."

Damien didn't answer.

He opened his notebook.

Instead of taking notes, he drew six horizontal lines.

Then another six.

Beside them, he drew the shape of a guitar neck.

Outside, Dahlia kept playing.

Damien kept watching her hands.

---

After class, Damien walked out through the school gate with Ben, Joan, Elena, and Mateo.

They talked about something he wasn't listening to.

He was looking farther ahead.

Dahlia was walking toward the road on the other side of the street.

Her guitar was strapped across her back.

She was wearing a helmet.

A motorcycle waited beside her.

She climbed onto it, started the engine, and disappeared into the traffic.

Damien watched until the motorcycle became too small to distinguish from the others.

Then he stepped away from his friends.

"Where are you going?"

Ben asked.

"Home."

"We're going that way."

"I know."

Damien lifted one hand.

The air beneath his shoes tightened.

He rose before anyone could say anything.

Joan looked up.

"Show-off."

Damien was already above the trees.

The houses below became smaller.

The road stretched ahead in long pieces of concrete and asphalt, crossed by wires and shaded in places by acacia and mango trees.

He flew low.

The wind pressed his school shirt against his back.

For a while, he followed the road.

Then he saw the motorcycle again.

Dahlia was farther ahead.

He watched her turn toward another road.

Damien stopped in the air.

She continued alone.

He changed direction.

---

He landed behind his house.

The back door was open.

His mother was in the kitchen.

Damien walked inside, left his bag on his bed, and removed his shoes.

He didn't change his uniform.

He went upstairs.

Then farther up.

The attic door groaned when he opened it.

Dust covered the floorboards. Old boxes leaned against one another beneath the sloping roof. A broken electric fan sat in the corner beside a stack of newspapers his father had never thrown away.

Damien moved one box.

Nothing.

Another.

Nothing.

He pushed aside an old blanket.

There.

A guitar case.

He pulled it toward him.

The hinges complained.

Dust broke apart across the lid.

Damien opened it.

An old guitar lay inside.

Its wooden body had several cracks along the side. The strings were yellow with age.

Damien lifted it carefully.

One of the strings broke beneath his fingers.

The sound was small.

It vanished into the attic.

His mother's voice arrived in his head from downstairs.

*Looking for something?*

Damien looked toward the ceiling.

*Just checking something.*

There was silence.

Then:

*You found something.*

Damien looked at the guitar.

*Maybe.*

He sat on the floor.

The wood was dry against his fingers.

He traced one of the cracks.

Then he held the guitar in both hands.

The air around his fingers changed.

Pearlescent light gathered against the wood.

The crack closed.

Another followed.

A third.

The guitar became whole again.

Damien smiled.

He turned it over.

The strings remained.

Except for one.

He touched the broken end.

Nothing happened.

He tried again.

The pearlescent light appeared.

Then vanished.

The string stayed broken.

Damien frowned.

He knew how to make metal.

He knew how to make wood.

He could make a wrench or a knife or a folder thin enough to catch a piece of paper before it hit the floor.

But the string was different.

It had to be a certain length.

A certain thickness.

A certain tension.

His fingers were not enough to guess it.

He looked at the guitar.

"It's not broken, though."

His mother's voice came again.

*What's that?*

Damien realized he had said it aloud.

"Nothing."

A sound came from outside.

A car engine.

Damien froze.

He knew that engine.

His father's car.

He picked up the guitar.

The attic suddenly felt too small.

He carried the guitar downstairs, ran to his room, and shoved it beneath the bed.

Then he closed the bedroom door.

His father had not entered yet.

Damien sat on the edge of the bed.

His breathing settled.

A few seconds later, the front door opened.

Shoes crossed the floor downstairs.

His father's voice followed.

"Where is everybody?"

His mother's voice answered.

Damien looked beneath the bed.

The guitar was hidden in darkness beneath the wooden frame.

He stared at it.

A few minutes earlier, it had only been an old instrument.

Now it was something else.

He didn't know what yet.

But he knew he wanted to learn.

Outside his room, his father's footsteps came closer.

Damien pushed the guitar farther under the bed.

Then he opened his school notebook.

On the last page, beneath a half-finished drawing of a road, he drew six lines.

Again.
