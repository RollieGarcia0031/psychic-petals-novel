# Chapter 1: Unlabeled Maps

The classroom was louder than Sir Kent had expected.

He stood beside the teacher's desk with a stack of papers tucked beneath one arm, watching thirty-seven unfamiliar
faces talk over one another as though the school had given them a single morning to exchange every name, address,
rumor, and plan they had collected over the summer.

He had been assigned as their class adviser only because their regular adviser was unavailable.

For the year, apparently, he would be the person expected to know which student was absent, who had forgotten a
form, whose parents needed to be contacted, and which child had somehow managed to lose the same school ID three times.

He knew none of them.

Not yet.

So he waited until the noise had settled into something he could work with.

"Alright."

A few students continued talking.

He knocked his knuckles against the desk.

"Alright."

This time they looked up.

Sir Kent adjusted his crooked tie.

"Since I'm apparently your adviser for now, I should probably learn something about you people."

A few students laughed.

"Names aren't enough. I'll forget half of them by lunch."

More laughter.

He picked up a marker.

"So we're doing something simple."

He pointed toward the empty sheets on their desks.

"Draw a map from the school to your house. Doesn't have to be accurate. Roads, stores, trees, landmarks. Whatever
you remember."

Someone immediately asked, "Can I draw my whole barangay?"

"If that's where you live."

"What if I don't know the roads?"

"Then draw what you know."

He paused.

"And somewhere on the map, put something you want to reach someday. A place, a job, a dream, whatever."

A boy near the back raised his hand.

"Sir, can I put a girlfriend?"

"If that's your dream."

The class laughed.

Sir Kent pointed the marker at him.

"Just don't draw her with a different face every five minutes."

The room loosened.

Paper moved across desks.

Pencil cases opened.

Someone borrowed an eraser and never returned it.

Sir Kent walked between the rows, looking over shoulders without reading too closely. One student had drawn a
hospital. Another had drawn a basketball court. Someone had already labeled a restaurant they apparently intended
to own.

Then he reached Damien's desk.

The boy hadn't started.

Sir Kent glanced at him.

"You're still thinking?"

Damien looked down.

"No."

He reached into his bag and pulled out a sheet of paper.

It had already been folded.

Sir Kent moved on.

Damien unfolded it.

The creases remained in the paper, crossing one another in a neat grid.

He began with the road.

He knew it without needing to think.

The school gate.

The narrow stretch beside the drainage canal.

The corner where the pavement changed from gray concrete to patched asphalt.

The sari-sari store with the faded sign.

The tricycle terminal.

The vacant lot.

The acacia tree that had grown too close to the road.

His pencil moved steadily.

He drew the houses along the way, giving each one the details he remembered: windows, fences, roofs, water tanks,
laundry hanging beneath the eaves.

The map grew larger.

Sir Kent glanced at it once as he passed.

Then he looked again.

Damien was drawing the road in perspective.

Not roughly.

Precisely.

The houses had depth.

The trees had branches.

Even the shadows fell in the right direction.

He continued until the road reached the last turn.

His neighborhood.

His street.

His gate.

He drew the fence.

He drew the trees behind it.

Then stopped.

There should have been a house.

There wasn't.

The space remained blank.

Damien looked at it for a long time.

Then he set his pencil down.

He had not labeled anything.

Dahlia noticed.

She had finished her own map and was now leaning over the aisle to see what everyone else had drawn.

Her map was bright with colored pencils. Buildings had names. There were arrows everywhere. One corner contained
a small cat.

She stopped beside Damien's desk.

"Nice map."

Damien looked up.

Dahlia pointed at the blank space.

"Where's your house?"

He looked at the paper.

Then at the trees.

"Somewhere here."

Dahlia leaned closer.

"Somewhere?"

"Yeah."

She looked at the map again.

There were roads.

Trees.

Fences.

Every house along the way.

Only his was missing.

"Interesting."

She said it without laughing.

Then she returned to her seat.

Sir Kent looked toward the front row.

"You."

Damien looked up.

"Me?"

"Yes, you. Come here."

Damien stood.

He carried the map to the front.

Sir Kent held out his hand.

Damien gave it to him.

The teacher studied the drawing.

"That's quite a map."

Damien said nothing.

Sir Kent looked toward the class.

"Alright. Tell us about it."

Damien pointed to the road.

"I live near the school."

"Where?"

He pointed at the trees.

"Here."

Sir Kent glanced at the blank space.

"Okay."

He handed the map back.

"And what did you put for where you want to go?"

Damien looked down.

He hadn't put anything.

Sir Kent waited.

Damien waited too.

The classroom became quieter.

He could feel everyone looking at him.

"My dream is..."

He stopped.

The answer he had imagined earlier that morning had disappeared.

There had been several.

None of them seemed appropriate anymore.

He looked at the map in his hands.

"I'm kinda working on it."

Someone laughed.

Then Dahlia did.

It wasn't cruel.

That made it worse.

Damien returned to his seat.

Sir Kent smiled.

"Fair enough."

He turned back toward the class.

"Anyone else want to share?"

A hand rose from the middle row.

"Sir."

"Yes?"

"What subject do you actually teach?"

The room laughed.

Sir Kent put a hand against his chest.

"Math."

A collective groan.

"I'm your class adviser today," he said, "not your math teacher for this activity. But yes, unfortunately, I do
teach math."

Someone shouted, "Give us a math fact!"

"Why?"

"Because we're bored!"

Sir Kent thought about it.

"A math fact."

He tapped his marker against his palm.

"Did you know the first mathematician was mentioned in the Bible?"

The class quieted.

Sir Kent looked serious.

"It was Moses."

A few students frowned.

"He invented the first equation."

He wrote:

**C / 2**

"The Red Sea divided by two."

There was a second of silence.

Then the room erupted.

Even the students who had spent the morning pretending not to care laughed.

Damien didn't.

He looked at his map.

His thumb rested on the road leading toward home.

The road ended among the trees.

He traced it once.

Then again.

"Alright," Sir Kent said. "Since we're done pretending this is productive, take a break."

Chairs scraped across the floor.

Students spilled into the hallway.

Damien stayed.

He drew a small tree beside the road.

The classroom became quiet enough for him to hear the electric fan clicking overhead.

A few minutes later, the door opened.

Dahlia stepped inside carrying a small milk carton.

She stopped when she saw him.

"Mind if I sit here?"

She pointed to the empty chair beside him.

Damien looked at it.

"If you want."

She sat.

For a while, neither spoke.

Damien continued drawing.

Dahlia drank from her carton.

Then she leaned forward and squinted at the board.

The sunlight coming through the window had turned half the writing into a green blur.

She shifted her chair.

Nothing.

She leaned sideways.

Still nothing.

"I think I'll go back."

Damien looked up.

"Easier to see from there."

"Yeah."

She gathered her things.

Damien watched her leave.

He had chosen this seat because of the glare.

From here, half the board was difficult to read.

The angle also kept him slightly behind everyone else's attention.

It was a good seat.

Nobody wanted it.

He returned to his drawing.

The rest of the morning passed.

When the school dismissed them early, Damien packed his things and walked home.

Students filled the road outside the gate, gathering in groups beneath the trees, buying snacks, calling for rides,
complaining about teachers they had known for less than a day.

Damien walked between them.

Nobody called his name.

He preferred it that way.

---

The next morning, Damien arrived early enough to hear the janitor unlocking the hallway.

The school was still cool.

The floors smelled faintly of disinfectant, and somewhere near the office a radio played quietly beneath the voices
of teachers preparing for the day.

He entered the classroom.

Someone was already sitting beside his desk.

A boy with messy hair and thick-rimmed glasses was drawing circles in the corner of a notebook.

He looked up.

"Hi."

Damien stopped.

"My name is Ben."

Damien looked at him.

"I know."

Ben blinked.

"You do?"

"We were in the same history class."

"Oh."

Ben smiled.

"Right."

Damien put his bag down.

"You sat near the window."

"That's right."

"You laughed a lot."

Ben laughed.

"Yeah. I probably still do."

He moved his notebook aside.

"Can I sit here?"

Damien looked toward the window.

"Sure."

"Thanks."

Ben settled into the chair.

"The glare from my old seat is terrible."

Damien nodded.

For several seconds, there was silence.

Then Ben looked at him.

"So, do you live nearby?"

"Yeah."

"How far?"

"Twenty minutes."

"Walking?"

"Yeah."

"Every day?"

"Usually."

Ben whistled softly.

"I barely got here before the janitor."

Damien opened his notebook.

Ben smiled.

"I guess you're just weirdly early."

"Maybe."

The bell rang.

---

By the time biology started, Ben had already asked Damien enough questions to make Damien suspect he might eventually
run out of answers.

The teacher drew a diagram of the circulatory system.

Ben copied it.

Then he leaned toward Damien.

"You know what?"

Damien kept writing.

"I think I want to be a nurse."

Damien glanced at him.

"That's good."

"Except..."

Ben looked at the drawing of the heart.

"I'm scared of blood."

Damien looked at him.

"That's probably a problem."

Ben stared.

Then covered his mouth to keep from laughing.

Damien looked back at the board.

When class ended, Ben packed his things.

"I think I'll sit in the back next period."

"Okay."

"See you."

"See you."

Ben left.

Damien watched him go.

The classroom became quiet again.

It should have felt better.

It didn't.

---

On the third morning, the heat was already rising from the highway.

Damien walked along the shoulder with his bag hanging from one shoulder.

Jeepneys passed in bursts of noise.

A bus exhaled black smoke.

The pavement shimmered ahead of him.

He looked toward the school.

Still too far.

His fingers moved.

Something pale gathered beneath his skin.

He stepped off the pavement.

The air beneath his shoes became solid.

He rose.

Only a little.

One foot.

Then two.

He could have gone higher.

"Damien!"

He dropped immediately.

A motorcycle rolled onto the shoulder.

Ben lifted his helmet.

"What are you doing walking in this heat?"

"Going to school."

"Hop on."

"I'm fine."

"You're sweating."

"So are you."

Ben laughed.

"I have a motorcycle."

Damien looked at the bike.

The engine ticked beneath the morning heat.

Ben held out a spare helmet.

"Come on."

Damien took it.

The helmet smelled of sweat and road dust.

He put it on.

The motorcycle started moving.

Damien held Ben around the waist for the first few seconds before remembering himself and grabbing the rear handles.

Ben laughed.

"Dude."

"What?"

"Nothing."

They drove toward school.

The wind pressed against Damien's shirt.

The road blurred.

For once, he wasn't walking.

Ben shouted something over his shoulder.

Damien couldn't hear.

"What?"

Ben shouted again.

"What?"

Ben gave up.

They continued toward school.

---

Dinner was soup.

Damien sat near the stove.

His mother asked about school.

He told her it was fine.

She asked about classmates.

He told her there was a boy named Ben.

She smiled.

"Are you making friends?"

"Maybe."

"What does he do?"

"He talks."

His mother laughed.

Then Damien mentioned the motorcycle.

Her smile disappeared.

"A motorcycle?"

"He gave me a ride."

"Damien."

"I'm fine."

"Those things are dangerous."

"I know."

"Did he drive carefully?"

"Yes."

She continued looking at him.

Damien smiled.

"Mom."

"What?"

"If it's dangerous, I can just fly."

His mother's hand stopped.

She looked at him.

Damien continued eating.

After a moment she said, "Don't joke about that."

"I'm joking."

"I know."

She didn't sound convinced.

The front door opened.

His father came home.

The sound of his shoes crossing the floor was enough to change the room.

He sat at the head of the table.

His mother served him.

Nobody spoke for a while.

Then his father looked at Damien.

"School okay?"

"Yeah."

"Need anything?"

"No."

His father nodded.

They ate.

His mother's voice came quietly into Damien's thoughts.

*Don't ride with that boy again.*

Damien kept his eyes on his bowl.

*I said I'm fine.*

*I know.*

His father continued eating.

Outside, a jeepney rattled down the road.

Damien looked toward the window.

He thought about his map.

Every road had been easy to draw.

Every turn.

Every tree.

Every gate.

The house was the only part he couldn't bring himself to put on the page.

He lowered his eyes.

His soup was getting cold.

He ate it anyway.
