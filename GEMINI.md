CORE PRINCIPLE:
Magical realism does not explain its magic. The impossible is narrated in 
the same flat, matter-of-fact tone as the ordinary — a levitating grandmother 
gets the same sentence rhythm as a cup of coffee going cold. Never have 
characters react with shock, scientific curiosity, or "wait, how is this 
possible?" Only outsiders to the world's logic may react that way, sparingly.

VOICE RULES:
- Ground every magical moment in sensory, physical detail (smell, weight, 
  temperature, texture) before or instead of explaining mechanism.
- Let the magic carry emotional or thematic weight — it should mean 
  something about grief, memory, family, colonialism, desire, etc. It's 
  never magic for spectacle's sake.
- Prefer long, accumulating sentences with clauses that pile detail on 
  detail, alternated with short, blunt ones for impact. Avoid uniform 
  sentence length — that's the #1 tell of AI-generated prose.
- Avoid stock metaphors (moonlight silver, eyes like storms, hearts of 
  stone). Draw imagery from the specific world, region, and sensory palette 
  of this story's setting instead of generic "literary" imagery.
- Dialogue is oblique. Characters often don't say what they mean; let 
  subtext carry emotional truth. Avoid on-the-nose dialogue that states 
  feelings directly.
- Time can fold, repeat, or skip — treat this as normal narrative texture, 
  not a twist to be flagged.

WHAT TO AVOID:
- Do not summarize a scene's emotional meaning at the end of it ("And in 
  that moment, she understood..."). Trust the reader.
- Do not over-describe magical mechanics or give internal "rules" for how 
  the magic works — this is fantasy logic, not magical realism.
- Do not hedge or soften strange events with "as if" or "seemed to" — 
  state the impossible as fact.
- No generic scene-setting openers ("The sun rose over the quiet village 
  as..."). Start scenes in motion or mid-image.
